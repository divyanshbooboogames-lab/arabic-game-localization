# Cultural and Regulatory Rules for Publishing/Localizing Games in Middle East Arab Countries (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: 13 countries (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Yemen, Iraq, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, Palestine, Egypt). Research pass ran 2026-10-13. Labels used below: **[LAW]** = legal requirement with regulator or statute cited; **[ENFORCEMENT]** = documented regulator action; **[PRACTICE]** = cultural or industry best practice; **[OLD]** = rule dated before 2023 whose current status was not re-verified; **[NOT YET IN FORCE / TRANSITIONAL]** = announced or in a grace period. Source quality is flagged where it is weak (fan wikis, gambling-affiliate sites, search-engine summaries).

## 1. Regulators and age-rating systems per country; is IARC accepted; what do mobile games need?

### Takeaway
Only Saudi Arabia (Gmedia, formerly GCAM) and the UAE (National Media Authority, formerly UAE Media Council) run formal game age-rating systems. Both use the same six tiers: 3, 7, 12, 16, 18, 21. For digital/mobile releases both now work through IARC. Saudi Gmedia joined IARC in 2026, and the UAE does not require an NMA certificate for digital-only releases. Physical releases need a local certificate and local logo. The other 11 countries have no game rating board. Their regulators act through after-the-fact blocking instead: telecom regulators (Kuwait CITRA, Jordan TRC, Egypt NTRA), media regulators (Egypt SCMR, Iraq CMC) or parliament (Iraq).

### Cited Findings
**Saudi Arabia: General Authority for Media Regulation (GAMR, branded "Gmedia"; formerly GCAM, General Commission for Audiovisual Media)**
- [LAW] Rating tiers: 3, 7, 12, 16, 18, 21. The 21 rating was added in 2025. Microsoft's ratings table also lists a "TBC" (to be classified) marker. — [Microsoft Learn, Age ratings guidance (updated 2026-09-09)](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/game-publishing/concepts/age-ratings-guidance)
- [LAW] "In 2026, Gmedia joined IARC." Gmedia ratings for digital-only products on Microsoft storefronts are now generated through the IARC questionnaire. The Saudi store locale is AR-SA, with the note "Gmedia (via IARC)". — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/game-publishing/concepts/age-ratings-guidance)
- [LAW] Boxed/physical games: the Gmedia rating must appear on the front of the outer wrap. It is the only rating allowed on the box, and showing ESRB/PEGI logos is a violation. Point-of-sale materials must use the Gmedia rating, and a Gmedia certificate is required at disc certification. Microsoft recommends over-stickering ESRB with Gmedia on physical goods. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/game-publishing/concepts/age-ratings-guidance)
- Gmedia submission takes about 4 weeks. No game build is needed; submit video, screenshots and a game description. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/game-publishing/concepts/age-ratings-guidance)
- [LAW] Gmedia's e-service page says: the developer, publisher or local agent must submit; each video must be at least half an hour and cover the full game content (characters, levels, storyline); submissions must be in English or Arabic, otherwise translated into English. *Caveat: the direct fetch of gmedia.gov.sa failed (connection refused), so this comes from a search-engine summary of the official page.* — [Gmedia e-services: Age rating for video games](https://gmedia.gov.sa/en/services/age-rating-for-video-games)
- A secondary marketer guide says classification is mandatory and a title Gmedia declines to rate is "effectively banned from the market". It also says a valid import/distribution licence is required. *Secondary source with no statute cited.* — [Salman Gul, Gaming Age-Rating Rules in the GCC (2026)](https://salmangul.com/gaming-regulation-age-rating-gcc/)
- [NOT YET IN FORCE / status unclear] A Saudi draft Media Law (c. 2020–21) would bring "electronic games" under media licensing, with fines up to SAR 10 million (doubled for repeats), suspensions of up to 6 months and blocking. Its enactment status was not confirmed. — [DGA, New Law Governing Media in Saudi Arabia](https://dgagroup.com/insight/asg-analysis-new-law-governing-media-saudi-arabia/); [SMEX](https://smex.org/saudi-authorities-monopolize-media-and-advertising-discourse-by-law/)
- [LAW, 2026] A royal decree approved Saudi Arabia's Entertainment Activities and Supporting Activities Law. Penalties: a warning, fines up to SAR 1,000,000, a ban of up to 5 years, or licence cancellation. A violations schedule is still to be issued. Whether games/esports fall within scope is not stated. — [Bird & Bird (2026)](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/saudi-arabia/overview-of-saudi-arabias-new-entertainment-activities-and-supporting-activities-law)

**UAE: National Media Authority (NMA; renamed Jan 2026 from UAE Media Council; earlier NMC/MRO)**
- [LAW] Rating tiers: 3, 7, 12, 16, 18, 21. The UAE Media Council was established in February 2023 by Federal Decree-Law and renamed the National Media Authority in January 2026. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/game-publishing/concepts/age-ratings-guidance)
- [LAW] All physical games, including code-to-content cards, must be NMA-rated with the rating printed on the box. "Digital ONLY releases do not require a NMA rating", and IARC generic ratings are shown on the store. Store locale is AR-AE. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/game-publishing/concepts/age-ratings-guidance)
- [LAW, 2018 origin] The classification system was launched in 2018 by the National Media Council and covers games on console, PC, online platforms and mobile phones. Rating criteria: gambling (including simulated gambling), language, sex and nudity, alcohol/tobacco/drugs, violence and horror. Content that fails the guidelines may be rejected. — [The National (2018)](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/uae-adopts-new-classification-systems-for-all-films-books-and-games-1.706479); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/new-uae-ratings-system-for-films-games-books-1.2176427); [Gulf News, decoding UAE age ratings](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/decoding-uae-age-ratings-for-films-books-and-video-games-what-parents-should-know-1.1727272240932)
- [LAW, in force 29 May 2025] Media Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 55 of 2023) sets 20 binding media content standards, enforced under Cabinet Decision No. 42 of 2025. Violations fall into 4 severity degrees with fines of AED 5,000 to 1,000,000, doubled (up to AED 2M) for repeats within a year. Other penalties include closure for up to 6 months or permanently and licence revocation. The rules apply in free zones too. — [Clyde & Co (May 2025)](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/05/uae-media-regulations-framework); [The National (29 May 2025)](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/05/29/uae-reveals-new-media-rules-with-fines-of-up-to-dh2-million/); conflicting minimum fine of AED 10,000 in [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/uae-media-law-media-must-follow-20-key-standards-to-avoid-fines-of-up-to-dh1m-1.500157204)
- [LAW, in force 1 Jan 2026; TRANSITIONAL to Jan 2027] The Child Digital Safety Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 26 of 2025) applies to platforms "operating in the UAE or directed to users in the UAE", including online gaming and app-based services. "Child" means anyone under 18. Obligations:
  - "effective and reasonable age-verification mechanisms"
  - children may not access "online games involving gambling and betting"
  - restrictions on targeted advertising to children
  - no processing of data from under-13s without verifiable parental consent
  - "privacy-by-default settings for children's accounts"
  - platforms must "apply age ratings"
  - parental controls, including usage-time limits and mandatory breaks
  - reporting channels

  Full compliance is due by January 2027 unless a Cabinet decision extends it. Penalties will be set in implementing regulations. Regulators: TDRA plus the Child Digital Safety Council. — [Clyde & Co (Jan 2026)](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/01/uae-issues-landmark-child-digital-safety-law); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/uae-introduces-new-child-digital-safety-law-what-online-platforms-need-to-know); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/united-arab-emirates/regulating-the-uaes-virtual-playground-new-child-digital-safety-law)
- [ENFORCEMENT] The UAE's telecom regulator, TDRA, not the media regulator, made the September 2025 child-safety deal with Roblox. — [The National (4 Sep 2025)](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/09/04/uae-clamps-down-on-regulation-of-video-game-roblox/)

### Inferences
- For a mobile casual game shipped digitally through Google Play / App Store, the practical requirement in every one of the 13 countries is a correct IARC questionnaire. IARC generates the Gmedia rating for Saudi Arabia (from 2026) and generic ratings elsewhere. The age rating should be a per-locale field (SA: GMEDIA:{3,7,12,16,18,21}; AE: NMA/IARC-generic; others: IARC generic). Whether Google Play and Apple specifically surface Gmedia ratings via IARC was confirmed only for Microsoft's store. The other storefronts are an assumption.
- The UAE Child Digital Safety Law is the most consequential new obligation for casual mobile games with ads and IAP in the region. It brings age verification, a ban on gambling-like games for under-18s, targeted-ad restrictions, under-13 parental consent and playtime limits. Compliance is due by January 2027, so it is in a transitional period as of October 2026.
- In the 11 non-rating countries the risk is not rejection at submission. It is a sudden block by a telecom or media regulator, usually framed as child safety. Features most often cited: open user-to-user chat, user-generated content, and violence.

## 2. Content that triggers bans or rejections per country

### Takeaway
Documented bans and recalls cluster around five triggers:
- Quranic text or chanting used as entertainment audio (global recalls)
- LGBTQ+ themes (GCC-wide pressure on platforms)
- Depictions of regional political or historical trauma (Kuwait and Saddam Hussein)
- Violence/"addiction" (Iraq and Jordan PUBG bans; Saudi 2018 mass ban)
- Child-safety risks from open chat and user-generated content (the 2025–26 wave of Roblox bans)

The UAE's 2025 content standards are the clearest statute: they require respect for God, Islam and the Abrahamic religions, the state and national unity, and ban content inciting crime or drug use. Alcohol, pork, nudity and gambling appear as rating criteria (UAE) rather than outright bans in what was found.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW, UAE] The 20 media content standards (Federal Decree-Law 55/2023, effective 29 May 2025) require that content fully respect "the divine being, Islamic beliefs, and other Abrahamic religions". They also cover respect for the system of governance and state institutions, domestic and foreign policy, cultural heritage, national unity, and countering sectarian incitement and hate. They ban material inciting crimes including murder, rape, and drug or psychotropic use, and require privacy protection. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/05/29/uae-reveals-new-media-rules-with-fines-of-up-to-dh2-million/); [Gulf Today](https://www.gulftoday.ae/news/2025/05/31/uae-reveals-20-binding-standards-for-media-content-with-fines-of-up-to-dhs2-million)
- [LAW, UAE rating criteria] Gambling (including simulated gambling), coarse or sexual language, sex and nudity, alcohol/tobacco/drugs, violence and horror. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/new-uae-ratings-system-for-films-games-books-1.2176427); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/uae-adopts-new-classification-systems-for-all-films-books-and-games-1.706479)
- [Saudi, secondary] Gmedia sensitivities listed by a marketer guide: inappropriate religious language or symbols, sexual content (heavily restricted), violence (rated and sometimes moderated), content contrary to cultural or social norms, and gambling-like mechanics. *No statute cited.* — [Salman Gul (2026)](https://salmangul.com/gaming-regulation-age-rating-gcc/)

### Inferences
- Suggested per-locale content flags for all 13 locales:
  - Hard block on Quranic verses or recitation (text or audio) in any entertainment or music context
  - Hard block on LGBTQ+ romance or symbolism (rainbow pride iconography)

- "Magic/sorcery", alcohol and pork are widely cited culturalization risks. This pass found no regulator action against a game specifically for them, so they are best practice, not cited law (UAE rating criteria do cover alcohol).

## 3. Geopolitical naming: Arabian vs Persian Gulf, Palestine/Israel, maps, flags, country lists

### Takeaway
No game was found banned specifically over the Gulf's name, but the naming is a documented diplomatic flashpoint. Arab states use "Arabian Gulf" (الخليج العربي), and a Saudi-based federation cancelled the 2010 Islamic Solidarity Games over Iran's "Persian Gulf" logo. Treatment of Israel is a statutory issue in at least Iraq (2022 anti-normalisation law, death penalty) and Lebanon (1955 boycott law). Kuwait applies a primary boycott. Per-locale map and country-list rules are needed.

### Cited Findings
- The 2010 Islamic Solidarity Games (planned for Tehran) were cancelled after Iran used "Persian Gulf" on the logo. The Saudi-based federation cancelled the event. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/islamic-games-cancelled-over-persian-gulf-name-1.569378)
- Google Maps labels the body of water "Persian Gulf (Arabian Gulf)", reflecting Google's policy of showing both names when official names differ between countries. Iran threatened to sue Google in 2012 after the name was removed. — [Wikipedia: Persian Gulf naming dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_naming_dispute); [Cambridge Dictionary blog](https://dictionaryblog.cambridge.org/2012/06/18/political-geography); [Middle East Eye explainer](https://www.middleeasteye.net/explainers/persian-or-arabian-gulf-brief-history)
- [LAW, Iraq, May 26, 2022] The law "Criminalising Normalisation and Establishment of Relations with the Zionist Entity" passed 275 of 329 votes. It bans "diplomatic, political, military, economic, cultural and any other types of relations" with Israel and covers Iraqis anywhere, the private sector, the media, foreign companies and their employees. Penalties are death or life imprisonment. — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/5/26/iraqs-parliament-criminalises-normalization-with-israel); [AP via News4Jax](https://www.news4jax.com/news/world/2022/05/26/iraqi-lawmakers-pass-bill-criminalizing-any-ties-with-israel/)
- [LAW, Lebanon, 1955] Law No. 1/1955 on the Israel Boycott prohibits any natural or legal person from concluding agreements with persons residing in Israel or for its benefit, and bans Israeli goods. — [Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/law/Lebanon/Law_1_1955/en). A partisan 2026 commentary says enforcement is expanding (courts pairing it with "contact with the enemy" penal provisions). — [FDD (Apr 2026)](https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2026/04/27/if-boycotting-israel-requires-a-law-is-it-truly-popular/)
- [OLD, Kuwait] Kuwait applies a primary boycott of Israeli goods and services and keeps a boycott office in Customs. Under a 1994 GCC decision it no longer applies secondary or tertiary boycotts. — [USTR NTE report (2008/09)](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/uploads/reports/2009/NTE/asset_upload_file298_15453.pdf)
- [ENFORCEMENT] Kuwait blocked *CoD: Black Ops 6* over its Gulf War/Saddam Hussein depiction. Regional historical conflicts count as "geopolitical content", not just maps. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/kuwait-bans-new-call-duty-game-over-saddam-hussein-imagery-1973574)

### Inferences
- Pipeline rule for all 13 Arab locales: the body-of-water string should be "Arabian Gulf / الخليج العربي" (or neutral "the Gulf / الخليج"), never "Persian Gulf" alone. Iranian-market builds need the opposite, so this must be a per-locale string, not a global one.
- Israel/Palestine: for Iraq, Lebanon, Syria, Kuwait, Yemen, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman and Palestine locales, do not label a country "Israel" on maps, flags or dropdowns. Use "Palestine / فلسطين" or omit the territory. Do not include Israeli flags or Israeli-origin brand placements. Egypt and Jordan have peace treaties, and the UAE and Bahrain normalised relations in 2020 (Abraham Accords). *These diplomatic facts are general knowledge, not verified in this pass.* Even there, the cultural-risk posture for a consumer casual game makes omission or neutral handling the safe default. Avoid country dropdowns if possible; otherwise localise the list per locale.
- Other disputed items to flag per locale: Western Sahara (affects pan-Arab lists), the Gulf islands of Abu Musa and the Tunbs (UAE vs Iran), Kuwait–Iraq history, Hala'ib Triangle (Egypt/Sudan). *Not researched in this pass; flagged as known culturalization risks.*

- No current (post-2009) source on Kuwait's boycott enforcement. Syria's and Yemen's boycott laws not retrieved.
- Iraqi enforcement of the 2022 law against foreign media or apps not documented.

## 4. Monetization rules: gambling, loot boxes, gacha, spin wheels, lucky draws; IAP pricing and VAT display; consumer protection

### Takeaway
No statute or regulator decision in any of the 13 countries specifically addresses loot boxes or gacha. The risk comes from blanket gambling prohibitions rooted in Sharia (all GCC, Jordan, Iraq) and from prize-draw rules. Saudi law bans any purchase-linked "lottery" draw, and licensed competitions must not breach Sharia. The UAE is the outlier: since 2023 it has a federal commercial gaming regulator (GCGRA) licensing real-money gaming, and the 2025 Child Digital Safety Law bans children's access to gambling or betting games. VAT: Saudi 15%, UAE 5%, Bahrain 10%, Oman 5%, Egypt 14%, Jordan 16% GST, none in Kuwait and Qatar. Saudi requires VAT-inclusive price display identical to the invoice.

### Cited Findings
**Gambling / chance mechanics**
- [LAW, Saudi] The Ministry of Commerce prohibits "lottery" prize draws where buying goods is required to win or enter. Under the Anti-Commercial Fraud Law and its Executive Regulations, requiring a purchase to enter a commercial competition is illegal. Competitions need a licence and may not include anything violating Sharia or Saudi customs. Penalties: up to 3 years' imprisonment and SAR 1 million. 2024 enforcement: 9 firms summoned, 6 perfume retailers acted against during Ramadan, 44 businesses referred to prosecution. — [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/business/retail/saudi-arabia-bans-lottery-retail-prize-draws); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2571612/amp); [Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/641418/SAUDI-ARABIA/Commerce-ministry-penalizes-firms-organized-lottery-retail-prize-draws-to-promote-sales)
- [Saudi] Casinos and online gambling are prohibited under Sharia-based law. *Secondary source.* — [iGamingToday](https://www.igamingtoday.com/gambling-regulation-in-saudi-arabia/)
- [LAW, UAE, historical] Al Tamimi says federal and emirate laws "prohibit all forms of gambling, as well as the advertising of any form of gaming", including offshore online gambling offered to UAE residents. *Article appears dated (c. 2019).* — [Al Tamimi & Co](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/whats-the-bet-that-gambling-is-prohibited-in-the-uae/)
- [LAW, UAE, 2023+] The General Commercial Gaming Regulatory Authority (GCGRA) was established in September 2023. It defines "commercial gaming" as any game of chance, or chance plus skill, where money is wagered to win a reward. ## 5. Language requirements: Arabic store listings, EULA/privacy, in-game Arabic

### Takeaway
No country was found to legally require in-game Arabic for a digital game. Saudi Arabia has the strongest Arabic-language commercial rules: a 2012 Ministry of Commerce mandate for Arabic in bills, ads and contracts (English allowed as secondary); a Ministry of Commerce consumer guide requiring product data, specs and warranties in Arabic; and a National Arabic Language Policy (framework, not standalone enforcement). For apps these point to Arabic Terms, receipts and pricing as legal-risk reducers in Saudi Arabia. Gmedia accepts rating submissions in English or Arabic.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW, Saudi, OLD 2012] The Ministry of Commerce and Industry required Arabic as the primary language for utility bills, advertising, contracts and insurance certificates, with English allowed as secondary. Fines were up to SAR 100,000 (doubled for repeats) plus possible 1-year closure. Effective 15 Nov 2012. Current enforcement status not verified. — [Al Arabiya (2012)](https://english.alarabiya.net/articles/2012/09/17/238539); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/423810/amp); [Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/0001-01-01_378/en)
- [POLICY, Saudi] The Council of Ministers approved the National Arabic Language Policy. It "does not operate as a standalone enforcement instrument" but sets a framework for public, private and non-profit entities. Its principles call for Arabic in contracts, commercial signage, invoices and advertisements. Overseen by the King Salman Global Academy for the Arabic Language. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/sau/legal-updates/saudi-arabia-s-national-arabic-language-policy-what-businesses-need-to-know); [Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/national-policy-for-the-arabic-language-in-saudi-arabia)
- [LAW, Saudi] Ministry of Commerce consumer guide: product data and specifications must at least be written accurately, truthfully and clearly in Arabic, and technical specs and warranty details must be in Arabic where applicable. - Microsoft Store default locale languages: Arabic for Saudi Arabia (AR-SA), UAE (AR-AE) and Egypt (AR-EG); English for Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar and Jordan. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/game-publishing/concepts/age-ratings-guidance)

### Inferences
- Pipeline rule for SA: an Arabic store listing, Arabic Terms of Service/EULA, Arabic privacy policy and Arabic in-app purchase descriptions and price strings should be mandatory deliverables (legal-risk reduction). If both versions exist, the Arabic version will likely prevail in a Saudi dispute.
- For AE and EG (Arabic-default store locales), Arabic listings are strongly recommended. For BH, KW, OM, QA and JO, Arabic plus English is best practice.
- Any chat or UGC feature in any of these markets needs Arabic-language moderation, including dialect and Arabizi (Latin-script Arabic). Without it, a regulator block is a real risk.

### Gaps
- No source found confirming a current statute that requires Arabic EULAs/privacy policies for apps in Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt or any other listed country. The Saudi PDPL and Egyptian data-protection law language provisions were not researched.
- No source for Qatar Law No. 7 of 2019 on protecting the Arabic language (known to exist, not retrieved), or Jordan's 2015 Arabic language protection law. Both are potential [LAW] items and need verification.

## 7. Seasonal and cultural considerations: Ramadan, Eid, National Days, weekend, gestures, imagery

### Takeaway
Ramadan is the region's biggest live-ops window. Google Play and Tamatem case data show 48–50% IAP or promo revenue uplifts. Standard practice is to re-skin icons, store art and UI with crescent/lantern motifs, shift events and pushes to night hours after iftar, and add prayer-time awareness. Saudi regulators actively enforce prize-draw rules during Ramadan. No sourced findings were obtained for National Days, weekend structure, left-hand imagery, dogs/pigs or gestures; those remain best practice.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTICE] A 2021 Google Play case: Ramadan initiatives produced a 48% increase in IAP revenue and a 6% increase in DAU during Ramadan. — [Google Play Dev, "Go Global at Ramadan"](https://medium.com/googleplaydev/go-global-at-ramadan-eb181fcedae4)
- [PRACTICE] Tamatem expects about 50% revenue uplift on Ramadan LiveOps promotions. For Ramadan 2020 it changed the icon, store previews, loading screen, music, outfits and in-game decorations. Live ops shift from day to night, and some games send prayer-time alerts. — [Salaam Gateway](https://salaamgateway.com/story/covid-19-and-ramadan-super-prime-time-opportunity-for-the-middle-easts-mobile-game-developers)
- [PRACTICE] Examples:
  - PUBG Mobile: Ramadan lobby and a "Ramadan Kitchen" token event — [GamesMEA](https://gamesmea.com/pubg-mobile-brings-ramadan-vibes-to-the-game/)
  - Call of Duty: Mobile: "Starlit Quest" Ramadan event with regional creators, and a MENA Ramadan tournament — [tbreak](https://tbreak.com/call-duty-mobile-ramadan-starlit-quest/); [Call of Duty blog (2025)](https://www.callofduty.com/uk/en/blog/2025/02/call-of-duty-mobile-ramadan-celebration-announcement)
  - Mobile Legends: crescent and mosque motifs; EA FC Mobile: country-specific Ramadan campaigns — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/ramadan-festivities-unfold-across-mobile-with-themed-events-and-seasonal-rewards/)
- [ENFORCEMENT, Saudi] In 2024 the Ministry of Commerce acted against six perfume retailers over illegal lottery-style draws during Ramadan. Ramadan "lucky draw" promotions are an enforcement focus. — [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/business/retail/saudi-arabia-bans-lottery-retail-prize-draws)
- [PRACTICE] Gulf markets monetise well; Egypt has a large but lower-paying audience. — [Google Play Dev](https://medium.com/googleplaydev/go-global-at-ramadan-eb181fcedae4)

### Inferences
- Live-ops calendar rules per locale:
  - Ramadan dates follow the Hijri calendar and shift about 11 days earlier each year. Start dates can differ by a day between countries depending on moon sighting, so schedule per country.
  - Move event resets and push notifications to after iftar (sunset) and into the late night or suhoor window. Avoid daytime food or drink imagery and ad creatives showing eating or drinking while players are fasting.
  - Avoid romance-themed or party/music-heavy events during Ramadan.
  - Run Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha gift and reward events.
  - Ramadan promos in SA must not use purchase-linked prize draws.
- National Days to schedule per country (dates are general knowledge, *not verified in this pass*):
  - Saudi National Day: 23 Sep; Saudi Founding Day: 22 Feb
  - UAE: Eid Al Etihad, 2 Dec
  - Kuwait: 25–26 Feb; Qatar: 18 Dec; Bahrain: 16 Dec; Oman: 20 Nov (changed from 18 Nov in 2020, verify)
  - Others to confirm: Jordan Independence Day 25 May; Egypt: 23 Jul Revolution Day and 6 Oct Armed Forces Day; Iraq; Lebanon 22 Nov; Syria; Palestine; Yemen
  - Using another country's flag or colours on a National Day banner is a known culturalization error.
- Weekend: most GCC states use Friday–Saturday weekends. The UAE moved to a Saturday–Sunday weekend with a half-day Friday in 2022 (*general knowledge, not verified in this pass*). Friday midday prayer is the key low-engagement window; avoid major launches or maintenance at Friday noon.
- Imagery best practice (unsourced in this pass, [PRACTICE]):
  - Avoid pigs or pork and alcohol in food or reward icons
  - Treat dogs cautiously (pets inside homes, dogs licking people)
  - Avoid left-hand eating or giving
  - Avoid soles of shoes pointed at people or images
  - Avoid thumbs-up in some contexts, the "OK" sign and other gestures
  - Use modest clothing for characters (covered shoulders and knees as a baseline for female and male avatars)
  - No mosques or Kaaba as game levels or destructible objects
  - No Arabic calligraphy that could contain God's names on floors, shoes or disposable items

### Gaps
- No primary or vendor source retrieved (Keywords Studios, Geogrify/Kate Edwards culturalization guides) for the gesture and imagery rules above. They are listed as unsourced best practice.
- No regulator guidance found on advertising timing during Ramadan for games.
- National Day dates and the UAE weekend change were not verified with sources in this pass.
