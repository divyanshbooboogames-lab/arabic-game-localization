# Ship one MSA core, configure thirteen markets

To get Arabic quality close to 99% across Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Yemen, Iraq, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, Palestine and Egypt, a mobile publisher should not commission thirteen translations. It should ship **one Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) string table to all thirteen countries** and put every real per-country difference into an explicit locale configuration: digits, calendars, currency, weekend, age rating, content flags and feature flags. Dialect should be limited to a thin marketing and live-ops overlay for the Gulf, the Levant and Egypt. Players prefer MSA for in-game text, the market leaders ship MSA, and Apple has no per-country Arabic metadata locale at all. The current AI process is probably failing on context and engineering, not on the model. In the first WMT English→Arabic evaluation (2025, Egyptian Arabic), **GPT-4.1 scored 77.0 against 78.5 for the professional human reference** ([WMT25 General MT Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.22.pdf)). The defects common in shipped Arabic games come from elsewhere: Unity TextMeshPro does not join letters, concatenated strings cannot carry Arabic's six plural forms or its gender agreement, English "you" has no gender, glossaries are missing, and bidi rules reorder numbers and prices. Each of these has a known fix. "99%" also needs a definition before anyone can hit it. **An MQM score of 99 allows at most 10 severity-weighted penalty points per 1,000 words**, which means ten minor errors or two major ones, and no critical ones ([MQM Council](https://themqm.org/error-types-2/the-mqm-scoring-models/)). A native linguist should measure it on a sample of at least 1,000 words. Machines cannot certify that score for Arabic. The best LLM judges reach only about 0.55 segment-level accuracy, and automatic error-span detection tops out near 21% F1, against 78% agreement between two humans ([WMT25 Automated Evaluation Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.24.pdf)). The pipeline that works runs LLM translation fed with a glossary and context, rule-based QA on every string, a judge from a different model family for triage, native bilingual revision and in-build LQA, and an independent MQM audit at every milestone.

## One MSA string table serves all thirteen countries

The strongest player evidence points one way. A survey of **382 Arabic mobile gamers found a "strong preference for Modern Standard Arabic over colloquial dialects"** ([UAEU Research portal](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/arabic-mobile-game-localizations-gamer-profiles-preferences-and-i-2/)). A 2022 study found that most Arab gamers prefer "partially localized" games (Arabic text without Arabic dubbing) to fully voiced ones ([UAEU](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/current-trends-in-localizing-video-games-into-arabic-localization/)). Industry practice matches. PUBG Mobile is the region's top-spending mobile game. It ships MSA in-game, with GCC-specific events and a regional community team added on top ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2567682/amp)). Belka Games' Solitaire Cruise was translated into MSA because "MSA is understood in all Arab countries", and its style guide explicitly bans dialect forms ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/cruise-to-the-east-how-to-successfully-localize-a-game-into-arabic/)). Altagram notes that text localization "tends to always be done in MSA" because MSA scales and gives a standard quality metric ([Altagram](https://altagram.com/challenges-in-arabic-localization-altagram/)). No public evidence shows a major publisher shipping separate ar-SA and ar-EG in-game string sets. Players punish poor quality, not missing dialect. In a survey of 1,225 GCC players, **41% play only, or more readily, in Arabic, and 27.4% might quit a game over inadequate localization** ([GameDev Reports on Niko Partners/SEF](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/niko-partners-and-saudi-esports-federation?open=false)).

Dialect still has a role, but a narrower one. Altagram recommends a neutral "White Arabic" for marketing and dialect for dubbing, and warns that high-register MSA can alienate players in casual and fantasy settings. It rates Egyptian as the most widely understood dialect and Gulf Arabic as the right choice for projects aimed at the Gulf ([Altagram](https://altagram.com/challenges-in-arabic-localization-altagram/)). A Dubai agency says gaming creative responds to Khaleeji dialect, and it claims Arabic-first creative doubles CTR over English ([Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/mobile-marketing-agency-dubai/)). Arabic-first titles held **12–22% of Saudi Arabia's iOS Top 50 in every quarter from July 2022 to April 2026**, under a vendor's broad definition: titles with Arabic names or from MENA-based studios ([Antidote](https://antidote.gg/how-localized-mobile-games-capture-6-10x-more-ios-share-in-saudi-arabia/)). These are agency and vendor claims. No published A/B test compares MSA with dialect for push notifications, UA creatives or store copy in any of the thirteen countries. Dialect spend is therefore a hypothesis to test, not established practice. The register map below records that evidence as an attribute on each string rather than as a per-country fork, because most strings stay MSA everywhere.

| Content type | Register | Who gets it | Evidence base |
|---|---|---|---|
| UI, buttons, system messages, IAP, legal, store title and keywords | Plain MSA | Single `ar` table, all 13 countries | Player survey, Solitaire Cruise, Altagram; search keywords must match across countries |
| Tutorials | Short, low-register MSA | `ar` | Altagram's warning about high register |
| Text dialogue | "Light MSA" (MSA grammar, simple vocabulary, a few common interjections) | `ar`; full dialect only for a single-market title | Altagram; Microsoft allows an "informal, playful" tone for games ([Microsoft ara-SA style guide](https://download.microsoft.com/download/5/6/d/56d1774f-ac3f-4ad9-80af-a6dfd5df419d/ara-sau-StyleGuide.pdf)) |
| Voice-over | Dialect or a pan-regional "white" voice; low priority | Per dialect cluster | 2022 preference for partial localization |
| Push, UA creatives, promotional text, screenshots | White Arabic or a dialect overlay | Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine overlays | Agency and vendor opinion only; needs testing |

If dialect overlays exist, cluster them the way Arabic NLP does: Egypt, Gulf, Iraq, Levant, Maghreb. Classifiers cannot reliably separate closely related dialects, and identical sentences recur across dialect classes ([Olsen et al., ArabicNLP 2023](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2023.arabicnlp-1.30)). That gives four string sets: an MSA master for all thirteen countries, a Gulf overlay for Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman, a Levantine overlay for Jordan, Lebanon, Syria and Palestine, and an Egyptian overlay for Egypt. Iraq and Yemen fall outside the Gulf cluster, so they get MSA only. Keep this dialect axis separate from locale formatting. CLDR splits month names along a different line, grouping Iraq with the Levant. So ar-IQ means MSA strings plus Syriac month names, while ar-AE means Gulf marketing copy plus Latin digits and a Saturday–Sunday weekend.

The storefronts limit how much per-country text is possible at all. Google Play allows **up to 50 custom store listings, each of which can target specific countries** ([Google Play Console](https://play.google.com/intl/en-GB_ALL/console/about/customstorelistings)). They are the only way to tell apart same-language users in different countries, so they are how Saudi-flavoured copy reaches Saudi Arabia and Egyptian-flavoured copy reaches Egypt under one Arabic listing ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/what-are-custom-store-listings-and-how-to-use-them)). Apple offers a single "Arabic" metadata locale. Its Arab storefronts default to English (U.K.), with Arabic as an additional language, and Palestine and Syria are missing from its storefront table ([App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-store-localizations)). On iOS, per-country variation comes from two places: Custom Product Pages, which users reach only through URLs, ads and curated placements ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages)), and in-app events with per-country availability ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/offer-in-app-events/offer-in-app-events)). Inside the game, determine the market from the StoreKit storefront or the Play billing country first, then device region, then fall back to `ar`. Apple warns that the storefront can change at any time and should not be stored with customer data ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/storefront.md)). Spend the overlay budget where the money is. Saudi Arabia produced **59.1% of MENA-3 games revenue in 2024**. The UAE has the highest ARPU at $84.60. Egypt has the largest audience but an ARPU of only $3.39 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mena-3-games-market-set-to-reach-28bn-by-2029/); [Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-05-28-mena-gaming-market-entry-guide-2026/)). That puts the Gulf overlay first, with Egyptian creative second for volume UA.

## CLDR defaults disagree with what players should see

Formatting data should come from ICU/CLDR at a pinned version, but the defaults should not be trusted blindly. In **CLDR 48, twelve of the thirteen locales default to Arabic-Indic digits** (١٬٢٣٤٫٥٦) with Arabic separators. Only ar-AE and generic `ar` default to Latin digits ([CLDR 48](https://cldr.unicode.org/downloads/cldr-48); values observed in an ICU 78.3 runtime). Microsoft's Saudi Arabic style guide says the opposite: "always use the Western Arabic Numbers (1, 2, 3…)". The W3C's Arabic layout requirements, meanwhile, record Arabic-Indic digits in use in Egypt, Saudi Arabia and Iraq ([Microsoft ara-SA style guide](https://download.microsoft.com/download/5/6/d/56d1774f-ac3f-4ad9-80af-a6dfd5df419d/ara-sau-StyleGuide.pdf); [W3C alreq](https://www.w3.org/TR/alreq/)). No player survey settles the question country by country. Treat it as a product decision and make it explicit: pin `-u-nu-latn` for HUD values (scores, timers, coins, levels) in all thirteen locales, and offer `arab` as a per-locale override for formal text.

Leaving the choice implicit creates two traps. A device that reports bare `ar` falls back to Latin digits while ar-SA gets Arabic-Indic, so neighbouring players see different digits in the same build. And Arabic-Indic digits have a different bidi class from European digits, so the choice also changes how numbers reorder next to Arabic text ([W3C alreq](https://www.w3.org/TR/alreq/)). CLDR defaults have already changed between versions: generic `ar` used native Arabic-script digits in 2019 ([CLDR-users](https://corp.unicode.org/pipermail/cldr-users/2019-April/000917.html)). Snapshot-test formatted strings per locale, so that a CLDR upgrade shows up as a test diff rather than a change players see.

Dates and weeks need the same care. CLDR 48 defaults to the **Gregorian calendar in all thirteen locales, including Saudi Arabia**, which moved government dealings to Gregorian in 2016 ([Anglican Ink](https://anglican.ink/2016/10/08/saudi-arabia-adopts-gregorian-calendar)). Hijri is opt-in, and the Umm al-Qura and tabular Islamic calendars can disagree by a day: for 13 October 2026, CLDR gives 2 Jumada al-Awwal 1448 under Umm al-Qura and the 1st under the tabular calendar. Use Gregorian everywhere. Show Hijri only for religious events, using `islamic-umalqura`. Drive Ramadan and Eid start times from server config, because moon-sighting announcements vary by country.

Month names split two ways. The Gulf, Yemen and Egypt use transliterated forms (يناير). Iraq, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria and Palestine use Syriac forms (كانون الثاني). All thirteen use a 12-hour clock with ص/م ([CLDR 48](https://cldr.unicode.org/downloads/cldr-48)).

Weekends are no longer uniform. The **UAE federal government moved to a Saturday–Sunday weekend with a Friday half-day from January 2022**, and Sharjah takes Friday through Sunday off ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/government/new-uae-weekend-and-working-hours-in-2022-all-your-questions-answered); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/education/new-uae-weekend-sharjah-private-schools-to-have-three-days-off-1.84348363)). Saudi Arabia has used Friday–Saturday since 2013 ([Fox News/AP](https://www.foxnews.com/world/saudi-arabia-moves-to-friday-saturday-weekend-to-align-with-region)). A "weekend event" therefore has to read from per-country config. CLDR's Saturday–Sunday weekend for Palestine looks doubtful and should be checked locally before use. If a Unity project formats dates through .NET `CultureInfo`, check what ar-SA produces on each target platform. The .NET Umm al-Qura calendar only covers 1900–2077 ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.globalization.umalquracalendar)), and it has not been verified whether IL2CPP builds default ar-SA to a Hijri calendar.

Currency is where formatting bugs cost money. Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman and Jordan use **three decimal places**. Yemen, Iraq, Lebanon and Syria use **zero**. CLDR 48 still puts abbreviations such as ر.س. after the number ([CLDR 48](https://cldr.unicode.org/downloads/cldr-48)). The Saudi riyal got its own sign, **U+20C1, in Unicode 17.0 (September 2025)** ([Unicode blog](https://blog.unicode.org/2025/03/support-for-new-saudi-riyal-currency.html)). The UAE dirham (U+20C3) and Omani rial (U+20C4) signs arrived in **Unicode 18.0 in September 2026** ([The National](https://thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/09/22/uae-dirham-symbol-keyboard-unicode)). Both central banks require the sign to go before the number ([Wikipedia: Saudi riyal sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_riyal_sign); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/how-to-use-the-new-uae-dirham-symbol-the-right-way-spot-incorrect-use-1.500160926)), but CLDR still treats U+20C1 as an alternate symbol and font support lags. To show the new signs, bake them into your own font atlas and place them before the number. Otherwise keep the CLDR abbreviations.

For real-money prices, show the store's own localized string (Play Billing `formattedPrice`, StoreKit `displayPrice`) instead of formatting the price yourself. Solitaire Cruise shipped a bidi bug that displayed **$4.99 as $99.4** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/cruise-to-the-east-how-to-successfully-localize-a-game-into-arabic/)). Some CLDR currency data is worth overriding. It names the Lebanese currency جنيه لبناني, and its narrow symbol for the Syrian pound is a bare £. It also defaults Palestine to the Israeli shekel, though store prices come from the storefront regardless.

Encode only the fields where policy overrides CLDR, and let everything else come from the pinned data:

```json
"ar-SA": { "strings": "ar", "overlay": "gulf", "nu": "latn", "calendar": "gregory",
           "hijri": "islamic-umalqura",
           "currencySign": { "codepoint": "U+20C1", "position": "before", "requiresFont": true } },
"ar-AE": { "strings": "ar", "overlay": "gulf", "nu": "latn",
           "weekend": ["sat", "sun"], "fridayHalfDay": true,
           "currencySign": { "codepoint": "U+20C3", "position": "before", "requiresFont": true } }
```

Terminology has the weakest evidence of all. No sourced country-by-country comparison of gaming vocabulary exists. The practical answer is one locked MSA glossary, reviewed by native speakers from each dialect cluster, with loanword slang kept for the marketing overlays. Microsoft's style guide gives the conventions that matter most for game counters and buttons: zero becomes a negation (لا يوجد), one is spelled out, two takes the dual (so "Spin Arrows" is سهمان, not أسهم), Arabic punctuation (، ؛ ؟) replaces Latin punctuation, and a no-break space goes between a number and its unit ([Microsoft ara-SA style guide](https://download.microsoft.com/download/5/6/d/56d1774f-ac3f-4ad9-80af-a6dfd5df419d/ara-sau-StyleGuide.pdf)).

## Regulators block chat, religion and gambling, not dialect

Only Saudi Arabia and the UAE run formal game rating systems, and both use **tiers of 3, 7, 12, 16, 18 and 21** ([Microsoft Learn: Age ratings guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/game-publishing/concepts/age-ratings-guidance)). Saudi Arabia's Gmedia joined IARC in 2026, so digital ratings for the Saudi store now come from the IARC questionnaire. The UAE does not require a National Media Authority certificate for digital-only releases. Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman and Egypt use IARC generic ratings, and Jordan shows ESRB ratings via IARC. For a digital mobile game, an accurate IARC questionnaire is in practice the whole submission requirement. That Gmedia ratings flow through IARC has only been confirmed for Microsoft's store, not for Google Play or the App Store. Lebanon, Syria, Yemen and Palestine have no documented game rating or classification rules.

The real risk is being blocked after launch, and since 2025 the blocks have followed one pattern: child safety on social and user-generated-content platforms. Qatar and Kuwait blocked Roblox in August 2025 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/2025/08/27/is-roblox-safe-as-gulf-countries-ban-platform/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/roblox-blocked-in-kuwait-over-child-safety-and-content-concerns-1.500240710)), Iraq followed in October 2025 ([NewsNation](https://www.newsnationnow.com/business/tech/iraq-bans-roblox-child-safety/)), and Egypt's media regulator ordered a block that took effect on **4 February 2026** ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2026/02/07/roblox-open-to-talks-following-egypt-ban-over-safety-concerns/)). Where Roblox negotiated instead, with the UAE's telecom regulator in September 2025, the fix was functional: **in-experience chat was temporarily disabled across a dozen Arab markets, and Arabic-language moderation was tightened** ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/09/04/uae-clamps-down-on-regulation-of-video-game-roblox/)).

Earlier bans targeted violence and "addiction". Iraq's parliament voted in 2019 to ban PUBG and Fortnite and the payments linked to them ([The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/iraq-parliament-unanimously-bans-pubg-fortnite-inciting-violence)). Jordan's telecom regulator banned PUBG ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/?p=177245)). Saudi Arabia banned 47 titles in 2018 ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Fgulf%2Fsaudi%2Fsaudi-arabia-bans-video-games-after-childrens-deaths-1.2252544)). For a casual title, the lesson is to make open chat, friend requests from strangers, revealing avatar options and user uploads into per-locale feature flags that ship off or restricted by default. If chat is on, staff Arabic moderation that covers dialect and Latin-script Arabizi.

The UAE carries the heaviest new legal load. Its **Child Digital Safety Law took effect on 1 January 2026, with full compliance due by January 2027** ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/01/uae-issues-landmark-child-digital-safety-law)). It covers any platform "directed to users in the UAE" and requires effective age verification, no access for under-18s to "online games involving gambling and betting", limits on targeted advertising to children, verifiable parental consent before processing data from under-13s, privacy-by-default settings, mandatory age ratings, and parental controls that include playtime limits. Its Media Law content standards, enforced since May 2025, require respect for "the divine being, Islamic beliefs, and other Abrahamic religions" and for the state. Fines run from AED 5,000 to AED 1 million and double for repeat violations ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/05/29/uae-reveals-new-media-rules-with-fines-of-up-to-dh2-million/); [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/05/uae-media-regulations-framework)).

Monetization law is blunter than it looks. No statute or regulator decision in any of the thirteen countries addresses loot boxes or gacha directly ([Blask loot-box map](https://blask.com/knowledge/loot-box/)). Saudi Arabia, however, bans purchase-linked "lottery" prize draws under its Anti-Commercial Fraud Law. Penalties reach three years in prison and SAR 1 million, and in 2024 regulators referred 44 businesses to prosecution, with Ramadan promotions among the targets ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/business/retail/saudi-arabia-bans-lottery-retail-prize-draws)). No one has tested whether a regulator would apply that law to virtual loot boxes, but an Islamweb fatwa already likens paid loot boxes to gambling ([Islamweb](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/379243/playing-video-games-with-loot-boxes)). The defensible Saudi setup is spin wheels that are free only, published drop rates, a deterministic route to every paid item, and no draws for real-world prizes. In the UAE, paid chance mechanics should be age-gated to 18+ before January 2027, even though the federal GCGRA now licenses real-money gaming for adults ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/gaming/1729372/uae-commercial-gaming-law-gcgra-licences-online-gaming-sports-wagering-and-lotteries)).

VAT is 15% in Saudi Arabia, 5% in the UAE and Oman, 10% in Bahrain, 14% in Egypt and 16% GST in Jordan. Kuwait and Qatar have none ([GCC Invoice](https://gccinvoice.com/blog/gcc-vat-rates-country-guide); [Anrok](https://www.anrok.com/vat-software-digital-services/egypt); [vatcalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/jordan/jordan-gst-on-digital-services-update/)). Saudi prices must be shown VAT-inclusive and must match the invoice ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1396251)), which is another reason to show the store's own price strings.

Content rules are consistent across the region, and documented recalls show where the hard lines are. Microsoft recalled *Kakuto Chojin* in 2003 because its background music contained a Quranic recitation ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250121-kakuto-chojin-microsoft-saudi-arabia)), and Sony re-pressed *LittleBigPlanet* worldwide in 2008 over two Quranic phrases in a licensed track ([GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/little-big-planet-recalled-planetwide-new-dates-issued/1100-6199562/)). GCC regulators told Netflix in 2022 to remove content contradicting "Islamic and societal principles" ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2157321/media)). *Call of Duty: Black Ops 6* was not approved for release in Kuwait in 2024, a decision widely tied to its depiction of Saddam Hussein and the Gulf War ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/kuwait-bans-new-call-duty-game-over-saddam-hussein-imagery-1973574)). At the casual end, Solitaire Cruise removed references to pigs from a live event and dressed female characters in outfits that cover the skin ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/cruise-to-the-east-how-to-successfully-localize-a-game-into-arabic/)). Tamatem replaced instrumental music with snapping and humming for part of its Saudi audience ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tamatem-has-built-a-thriving-mobile-business-by-bringing-games-to-the-arab-world/)). That shows content variants can be country-specific even when the language is shared. Alcohol, magic, dogs and gestures are standard culturalization cautions, but no regulator action against a game was found for any of them, so they belong in review rather than in hard blocks.

Geopolitical strings need per-locale handling. Use "the Arabian Gulf" (الخليج العربي) or simply "the Gulf", never "Persian Gulf" on its own. A Saudi-based federation cancelled the 2010 Islamic Solidarity Games over Iran's "Persian Gulf" logo ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/islamic-games-cancelled-over-persian-gulf-name-1.569378)). Iraq's 2022 law criminalizing normalization with Israel covers media and foreign companies and carries death or life imprisonment ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/5/26/iraqs-parliament-criminalises-normalization-with-israel)). Lebanon's 1955 boycott law bans agreements with people residing in Israel ([Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/law/Lebanon/Law_1_1955/en)). The safe default for a consumer game is no "Israel" label on maps, flags or country lists in Arab locales, and no country dropdowns at all wherever they can be avoided.

On language law, a 2012 Saudi Ministry of Commerce mandate made Arabic the primary language for contracts, bills and advertising; its current enforcement is unverified ([Al Arabiya](https://english.alarabiya.net/articles/2012/09/17/238539)). The ministry's consumer guide also requires product data and warranties in Arabic ([MoC consumer guide](https://mc.gov.sa/en/guides/Documents/CustomerGuide.pdf)). That makes Arabic terms of service, privacy policy, receipts and IAP descriptions required deliverables for Saudi Arabia, even though no country was found to legally require Arabic inside the game itself.

Seasonality is the commercial counterpart to these rules. Ramadan initiatives raised IAP revenue **48%** in a Google Play case ([Google Play Dev](https://medium.com/googleplaydev/go-global-at-ramadan-eb181fcedae4)). Tamatem plans for an uplift of about 50%: it re-skins icons, music and outfits and moves live ops to after iftar ([Salaam Gateway](https://salaamgateway.com/story/covid-19-and-ramadan-super-prime-time-opportunity-for-the-middle-easts-mobile-game-developers)). In Saudi Arabia those Ramadan promotions must avoid purchase-linked draws.

## AI Arabic breaks on missing context and engine shaping

If the current process produces poor Arabic, the model is probably not the bottleneck. In WMT25's English→Egyptian Arabic task, GPT-4.1 (77.0) and CommandA (74.0) came close to the human reference (78.5). Gemini 2.5 Pro scored 60.6, Claude 4 scored 55.7, and an anonymized commercial online MT engine scored 1.7, mostly because "systems tend to output Modern Standard Arabic" when Egyptian was requested ([WMT25 General MT Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.22.pdf)). That task weighted dialect compliance heavily, and no equivalent benchmark exists for MSA. In ATA certification exams scored by certified graders, GPT-4o passed English→Arabic on both texts while Google Translate failed one. For both systems the most common errors were terminology and literal translation ([Zou, Saeedi & Koby 2025](https://www.skase.sk/Volumes/JTI29/08.pdf)). That is a long way from 2023, when zero-shot GPT-3 scored 41 COMET against Google's 65.5 ([Moslem et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13294)).

The remaining failures are ones a model cannot fix from the string alone. The first is missing context: a playthrough review of the Arabic version of *Cyberpunk 2077* found at least 500 errors, many traced to translators lacking in-game context ([Saudi Digital Library](https://drepo.sdl.edu.sa/items/a726dbb5-08b3-416b-8f42-0813d3869749/full)). The second is string design shaped around English. The third is an engine that cannot draw Arabic.

### Grammar fails when strings arrive as fragments

Arabic uses **all six CLDR plural categories**, and each one takes a different construction, so a "{count} coins" template is wrong for most numbers ([CLDR plural rules](https://www.unicode.org/cldr/charts/latest/supplemental/language_plural_rules.html); [Kalimah Center](https://kalimah-center.com/arabic-numbers-grammar/)).

| Category | Rule | Grammar of the counted noun |
|---|---|---|
| zero | n = 0 | Negation phrase (لا يوجد…) |
| one | n = 1 | Singular, with the number spelled out (عملة واحدة) |
| two | n = 2 | Dual, with no numeral (عملتان) |
| few | n % 100 = 3–10 | Plural, genitive; the number takes the opposite gender |
| many | n % 100 = 11–99 | Singular, accusative |
| other | 100–102, 1000, decimals… | Singular, genitive |

Here is an illustrative ICU message for coins (عملة, a feminine noun). It shows the shape every count string needs, but it needs native review before use:

```
{count, plural,
  zero  {لا توجد لديك عملات}
  one   {لديك عملة واحدة}
  two   {لديك عملتان}
  few   {لديك # عملات}
  many  {لديك # عملةً}
  other {لديك # عملة}
}
```

Unity Localization's Smart Strings plural formatter follows CLDR and requires an `other` form ([Unity Localization 1.5](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.localization@1.5/manual/Smart/Plural-Formatter.html)), but its docs give no Arabic example. To confirm the branch order, preview every plural key at 0, 1, 2, 3, 10, 11, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103, 111, 1000, 1011 and 1.5. Code that does `count + " coins"` or `count == 1 ? … : …` next to a localization lookup should fail source lint.

Gender is the second structural trap. Arabic marks gender on second-person pronouns, verbs and adjectives, and the speaker's and addressee's genders have to be chosen independently. That is why the Arabic Parallel Gender Corpus exists ([APGC 2.0](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2022.lrec-1.199)). English "you" carries no gender, so models default to the masculine. The Arabic release of *Detroit: Become Human* was criticized for poor gender specification ([UAEU](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/issues-in-arabic-video-game-localization-a-descriptive-study/)).

The audience makes this costly. Women are **over 37% of MENA-3 players** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mena-3-games-market-set-to-reach-28bn-by-2029/)). Women made up most of the 382-player mobile survey ([UAEU](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/arabic-mobile-game-localizations-gamer-profiles-preferences-and-i-2/)). Even the GCC survey, which skews 69% male, implies close to a third of players are women ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/niko-partners-and-saudi-esports-federation?open=false)). A masculine imperative such as اضغط therefore addresses a large share of players in the wrong gender.

The fix has two layers. For UI, follow Microsoft's guidance: use verbal nouns (Solitaire Cruise used شراء for "Buy"), use collective nouns, and use يُرجى/يمكنك plus a verbal noun instead of imperatives ([Microsoft ara-SA style guide](https://download.microsoft.com/download/5/6/d/56d1774f-ac3f-4ad9-80af-a6dfd5df419d/ara-sau-StyleGuide.pdf); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/cruise-to-the-east-how-to-successfully-localize-a-game-into-arabic/)). For dialogue that has to address the player directly, store a player-gender setting and an ICU `select` branch, and store grammatical gender on item names so that agreement around `{item}` works. The QA tools need watching too. In WMT25's gender challenge set, Arabic showed some of the strongest masculine-favouring disparities among automatic raters ([WMT25 Automated Evaluation Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.24.pdf)).

Concatenation makes both problems worse. Arabic attaches articles and prepositions (الـ، بـ، لـ) directly to nouns, and verbs, adjectives and numbers all agree with the noun. So nobody can translate "You got " + item + " x" + n correctly. The rule is one full sentence per key, with named placeholders and the plural and select logic inside the message, related strings batched into one LLM call, and a QA warning wherever an Arabic letter is attached directly to a placeholder. Placeholders and rich-text tags must come through as an identical set, with the same counts, in source and target. Bare positional `%s %s` in a source string should be a source-side lint error, because it blocks the word reordering Arabic needs.

### Unity TextMeshPro still cannot join Arabic letters

The most visible defect in shipped Arabic games, disconnected and reversed letters, is an engine problem. TextMeshPro sends codepoints in stored order, so Arabic renders as isolated letters running left to right. `isRightToLeftText` reverses the order but joins nothing. A font with full Arabic coverage does not help either, because the problem happens before TMP receives the string ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-unity-localization-arabic-rtl-text-mirrored-wrong); [UnityGameTranslator #24](https://github.com/djethino/UnityGameTranslator/issues/24); [Allcorrect Games](https://allcorrectgames.com/insights/unity-from-right-to-left/)). **As of October 2026, uGUI with TextMeshPro still has no native Arabic shaping.** Unity's opt-in shaping for uGUI is targeted for roughly Unity 6.8 ([Unity Discussions, April 2026](https://discussions.unity.com/t/advanced-text-generator-atg-now-default-in-6-5/1715871)).

Two routes work today. For existing uGUI/TMP screens, use RTLTMPro. It shapes Arabic into presentation forms, supports multiline text, rich text and RTL input fields, and has a Unity 6 release (v4.0.0) in the hk1ll3r fork ([RTLTMPro README](https://github.com/pnarimani/RTLTMPro); [hk1ll3r releases](https://github.com/hk1ll3r/RTLTMPro/releases)). Its known traps belong on the QA checklist: the **"Farsi" checkbox converts digits to Persian numerals**, numbers with characters between them can swap ("2.5" becomes "5.2"), style tags can come out reversed, and the font atlas must include the Arabic Presentation Forms blocks and be at least 512×512 ([RTLTMPro issues](https://github.com/pnarimani/RTLTMPro/issues)). Store strings in logical order, unshaped, and never feed displayed TMP text back into save files, chat or analytics.

For new UI on Unity 6.3 LTS or later, build in UI Toolkit with the Advanced Text Generator. It uses HarfBuzz, ICU and FreeType for real shaping, bidi and line breaking ([Unity Manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/6/Documentation/Manual/UIE-advanced-text-generator.html)). It became the default runtime text system for UI Toolkit in 6.5, with its features backported to 6.3. It supports dynamic font assets only, and users still report bugs with RTL input fields and spacing ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/advanced-text-generator-atg-now-default-in-6-5/1715871); [Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/ui-toolkit-issues-with-rtl-input-and-advanced-text-generator/1707868)).

Other engines shape Arabic natively but fail on settings. Unreal's `KerningOnly` shaping "won't render complex right-to-left or bi-directional glyphs (such as Arabic) correctly", so Arabic widgets need `FullShaping` ([Unreal API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/SlateCore/ETextShapingMethod)). Godot 4's default TextServerAdvanced handles bidi and shaping, but it does not mirror sprites, and directional icons may need to be flipped by hand ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/i18n/internationalizing_games.html)).

Layout rules follow from the script. Arabic wraps only between words, and joined letters must never be spaced apart, so letter-spacing settings break Arabic text ([W3C alreq](https://www.w3.org/TR/alreq/)). Diacritics often sit outside a font's line box and get clipped on the top and bottom lines ([LibreOffice bug 119608](https://www.libreoffice.org/bugzilla/show_bug.cgi?id=119608)), so raise line spacing on Arabic font assets and avoid overflow masking. Published estimates of Arabic length relative to English range from 25% longer ([American Express guidelines](https://designinternet-qaeusw1green.americanexpress.com/guidelines/internationalization/localization)) to about 6% shorter ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=231)). Budget about 30% extra rendered width, and measure real strings in the engine rather than counting characters. Shipped AAA titles, from *The Witcher 3* to recent fighting games, still show defects in RTL layout, text direction and alignment ([UKM ejournal](https://ejournal.ukm.my/3l/article/view/61425); [UAEU](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/issues-in-arabic-video-game-localization-a-descriptive-study/)).

### Bidi rules silently reorder numbers and prices

Under the Unicode bidi algorithm, a European-digit number that follows an Arabic letter is treated as an Arabic number, and the separators next to it take on that type. That is why scores like "5-3", ratios, times and decimals get reordered inside Arabic sentences ([UAX #9](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr9/)). The fix, following W3C guidance, is to wrap every runtime value of unknown direction (player names, guild names, server-side item names, chat) in first-strong isolates (FSI…PDI), wrap known left-to-right fragments such as version strings, URLs and score lines in LRI…PDI, and avoid the legacy embedding controls ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-bidi-unicode-controls)). Insert these characters in code at format time, never in translation files:

```csharp
static string Isolate(string v)    => "\u2068" + v + "\u2069"; // FSI..PDI: player names, UGC
static string IsolateLtr(string v) => "\u2066" + v + "\u2069"; // LRI..PDI: "v1.2.3", "5-3", URLs
```

This works in engines that implement UAX #9: UI Toolkit's ATG, Unreal and Godot. RTLTMPro does not implement UAX #9, and TMP draws any codepoint missing from the font as □ ([TMP Settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.textmeshpro@4.0/manual/Settings.html)). In TMP builds, either add the control characters to the atlas as zero-width glyphs, or strip them and test mixed-direction text explicitly.

Encoding errors are the quieter problem. Persian ی and ک look identical to Arabic ي and ك, and Unicode normalization does not unify them ([W3C i18n archive](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-i18n-archive/2021JulSep/0253.html)). Enforce NFC, reject Persian letters and digits with a regex, and never apply NFKC, which destructively folds presentation forms and ligatures.

| Failure | Root cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong variety (MSA vs dialect) | Variety not specified; QE misses it | Register tag on each string; explicit variety check |
| Wrong or inconsistent terms, literal phrasing | No glossary or translation memory in the prompt | Locked glossary and fuzzy-match examples in the prompt |
| Wrong gender | English "you" has no gender; models default to masculine | Addressee metadata, verbal nouns, ICU `select` |
| Broken counts | English one/other plural logic | Six-category ICU plurals; test values |
| Ungrammatical sentences | Concatenated fragments | One sentence per key, named placeholders |
| Lost placeholders or tags | LLM edits; RTL plugin reverses tags | Placeholder and tag parity check before and after every edit |
| Disconnected, reversed letters | TMP does not shape Arabic | RTLTMPro or UI Toolkit ATG |
| □ boxes | Atlas lacks presentation forms or control characters | Check font coverage against all `ar` strings |
| Clipped diacritics | Marks sit outside the line box | More line spacing; no overflow masking |
| Reordered numbers, mirrored prices | UAX #9 number rules | Isolates at format time; store price strings |
| Persian letters or digits | Persian keyboard; RTLTMPro "Farsi" toggle | Regex rules; NFC enforcement |

## Ninety-nine percent means ten penalty points per thousand words

Only one framework gives "99% accuracy" a precise meaning. MQM gives errors default severity weights of 1 (minor), 5 (major) and 25 (critical) and normalizes penalties to 1,000 words. **A raw score of 99 is the MQM Council's own default pass threshold, equal to 10 minor or 2 major errors per 1,000 words** ([MQM Council](https://themqm.org/error-types-2/the-mqm-scoring-models/)). The same page sets legal translation at 99.5 and user-to-user technical help at 97.2, so 99 is strict but standard. With those weights, one critical error in 1,000 words scores 97.5 and fails, while one major error scores 99.5 and passes.

The definition to adopt is: an MQM raw score of at least 99 with 1/5/25 weights, where any critical error is an automatic fail, measured on a random sample of at least 1,000 source words per milestone, annotated by a qualified native Arabic linguist who neither translated nor post-edited the text. Define "critical" in the style guide in game terms: a broken placeholder that crashes or blanks a string, a wrong price, religiously offensive content, a forbidden map label. Spelling this out matters because Google's large-scale MQM work dropped the critical category, on the grounds that its definition depends on context ([Freitag et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14478)).

That score is not the same as "99% of strings correct", and game teams should report both. A game UI is thousands of short strings, so a table can pass MQM 99 per 1,000 words while dozens of individual strings are still wrong. By the standard rule of three, showing a string defect rate below 1% at 95% confidence takes about 300 randomly drawn strings with zero defects.

Sample size also drives volatility. The MQM Council's 2025 multi-range work finds pass/fail decisions on samples under about 250 words "statistically unreliable" and recommends Wilson or Agresti–Coull intervals for small samples. In one case, a linear model passed a 5,000-word text with 23 minor errors that expert reviewers rejected; a logarithmic model set the threshold at 16, matching the experts ([Gladkoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13467v1)). Keep the audit sample at the 1,000-word anchor, or switch to the non-linear model for long scripts. Otherwise milestone scores will swing for purely statistical reasons.

Covering thirteen countries works out cheaper than it sounds. Because one MSA table serves every country, the linguistic MQM audit runs once per milestone on the master, plus once per dialect overlay. The thirteen per-country passes are functional checks of formatting, legal text, content flags and rendering, and those are mostly deterministic and automatable.

Human annotation of Arabic is unusually consistent at the score level. WMT25's three professional English→Arabic annotators reached a pairwise correlation of 0.96 on a control subset, the highest of any language pair ([WMT25 General MT Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.22.pdf)). They agreed on only 78% of exact error spans, though ([WMT25 Automated Evaluation Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.24.pdf)). Before relying on a single annotator, calibrate two of them on a shared sample.

Automatic evaluators cannot certify the result. In WMT25's English→Arabic meta-evaluation, GPT-4.1 as a judge reached 0.86 pairwise accuracy at the system level but only **0.556 at the segment level** ([WMT25 Automated Evaluation Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.24.pdf)). At the segment level Claude 4 scored 0.398, MetricX-25 0.314 and CometKiwi-22 0.313, all below plain BERTScore at 0.656, and automatic error-span detection scored 5–21% F1. Other findings point the same way. LLM judges struggle to "assess near-perfect translations", which is exactly where a 99 target sits ([RUBRIC-MQM](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-industry.12/)), and they favour their own outputs ([Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.13076v1)). COMET gave GPT-4o and Google Translate the same score of about 0.97 on texts where certified graders failed Google ([Zou et al.](https://www.skase.sk/Volumes/JTI29/08.pdf)), and a system trained with xCOMET and GEMBA as rewards topped WMT25's automatic ranking but not the human one ([WMT25 General MT Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.22.pdf)). Use automatic raters for model selection, regression testing and routing segments to humans. Pick a different model family from the translator, and calibrate it on a few hundred human-annotated strings from the project.

Back-translation should be dropped as a quality gate. Round-trip scores correlate only weakly to moderately with quality ([Crone et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.00554)). The method is also blind by design to the most common Arabic game errors: a feminine imperative sent to a male player, the wrong register, or MSA where dialect was required all back-translate into correct English. Its only use is showing non-Arabic stakeholders what a string says.

## A gated pipeline spends human effort where machines are blind

The pipeline that reaches 99 does the cheap, deterministic fixes first and saves human judgment for what machines miss.

| Stage | Action | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Source internationalization | Full-sentence keys, named placeholders, ICU plural/select, speaker and addressee gender, character limits, screenshots, an RTL pseudo-locale | Source lint passes; pseudo-locale renders correctly |
| 2. Linguistic assets | Locked glossary and do-not-translate list; style guide covering register per content type, digits, punctuation, gender policy and the critical-error definition; translation memory | Native sign-off per dialect cluster |
| 3. LLM translation | Frontier model chosen by a bake-off; prompt with glossary hits, TM fuzzy matches, metadata and style guide; related strings batched; JSON output | Output parses; IDs and placeholders intact |
| 4. Rule QA | Every string: placeholders, tags, ICU completeness, Arabic rules | Zero errors; re-run after every edit |
| 5. LLM judge triage | Model from a different family in MQM-span mode, plus variety and gender checks; refine only flagged strings | Flags routed to humans |
| 6. Native revision | Bilingual revision by a second linguist: every string for UI, narrative, store and legal text; samples for the long tail; overlays by natives of each cluster | ISO 17100-style revision complete |
| 7. In-build LQA | Native testers on devices check shaping, RTL mirroring, truncation, clipping, plural test values, gender branches, per-locale formats and flags | Zero open critical bugs |
| 8. MQM audit | Independent annotator scores at least 1,000 random words per master and overlay; per-string defect rate tracked | MQM ≥ 99 with no critical errors; findings fed back into glossary, TM and prompts |

The prompt contents in stage 3 have the best-measured gains in the literature. Injecting up to five glossary terms raised **English→Arabic term adherence from 67% to 94%** and the professional quality score from 2.80 to 3.19 out of 4. Supplying translation-memory fuzzy matches as examples raised COMET from 41 to 64 with seven matches ([Moslem et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13294)). Those were 2023 models, so the gains on frontier models are probably smaller, but the direction holds. The same study warns that overlapping glossary terms can hurt grammar, so drop shorter terms that sit inside longer ones.

Refinement loops call for restraint. TEaR's translate–estimate–refine cycle added about 0.9 COMET ([TEaR](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16379v3)). But in WMT25's post-editing task the best LLM editor gained just 0.020 COMET, and several, including systems built on o3, made the output worse ([WMT25 Automated Evaluation Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.24.pdf)). Refine only the strings that the rules or the judge flag. A multi-agent translate–review–post-edit workflow performed best in 9 of 11 language pairs in Intento's 2025 evaluation, though it needed customization for each language ([Slator](https://slator.com/intento-releases-9th-annual-state-of-translation-automation-2025-industry-report/)).

Test model choice on the project's own strings, because rankings flip between test sets. Claude 4 trailed GPT-4.1 by 21 points on Egyptian Arabic in WMT25. Yet in a 2026 gaming-LQA study of eight LLMs, Claude Sonnet 4 had the best issue-detection F1 (0.766) ([Tian & Wu, AMTA 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.amta-research.12/)). That makes it a natural cross-family judge for a GPT-family translator, although that study did not confirm that Arabic was among its languages.

Rule QA in stage 4 is mature and cheap. Crowdin's built-in checks cover placeholders, tags, ICU syntax, numbers (including language-specific digits), length and terminology, plus custom AI-prompt checks ([Crowdin](https://support.crowdin.com/qa-checks/)). Xbench adds key-term and numeric mismatch checks ([Satsuma](https://www.satsuma.com.br/en/what-are-the-differences-between-xbench-2-9-and-3-0/)). Arabic needs about twenty more rules, and no off-the-shelf tool was found that bundles them. They should catch Persian letters and digits, presentation forms in stored text (a sign that pre-shaped text was pasted in), text stored in visual order, unbalanced isolates and legacy embeddings, Latin punctuation after Arabic letters, mixed digit systems, orphaned or stacked diacritics, decomposed hamza, text that is not NFC, tatweel, and Arabic letters attached directly to placeholders. Each is a short regex or Unicode test. Run them on every string before and after each LLM pass, alongside engine-side CI checks for font coverage, whether shaping was actually applied, line order, clipping and mirroring.

Human work in stages 6 and 7 cannot be skipped for a 99 target. ISO 17100 makes bilingual revision by a second person mandatory ([Wikipedia: ISO 17100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_17100)). The best LLM LQA detector still misses or misflags roughly a quarter of issues. Many Arabic defects only show up in the build: shaping, mirroring, truncation, or a gender branch that reads wrong after a player's choice ([Alconost](https://alconost.com/en/blog/game-localization-lqa)). Budget translation, revision, LQA and voice-over as separate line items. The only public price points are Egypt-based freelance game localizers asking about $0.08 per word ([ProZ](https://www.proz.com/pools/game-localizers/profile/59329857?print=1)). No agency publishes Arabic rates or LQA score distributions.

| Need | Recommended tools | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Translation model | Frontier LLM picked by bake-off (GPT-4.1 led WMT25 EN→AR; Gemini 2.5 Pro was in the top cluster in 14 of 16 pairs overall) | Rankings flip between test sets; re-test on your own strings |
| Judge | A different model family from the translator (for example a Claude Sonnet-class judge for GPT output), prompted for MQM spans | Triage only; calibrate against human MQM |
| TMS and QA | Crowdin (QA checks, ICU, AI check); Phrase (QPS scoring, Auto LQA); Smartling or XTM (MQM LQA scorecards); Xbench or Verifika | Phrase's "up to 80%" LQA saving is a vendor claim ([Phrase](https://phrase.com/blog/posts/understanding-phrase-quality-performance-score-phrase-qps-and-auto-lqa-how-they-unlock-hyperautomation-on-the-phrase-localization-platform)) |
| Arabic rules | Custom regex/Unicode checker plus an ICU parser (`@formatjs/icu-messageformat-parser`) | Tune the heuristics on your own string table |
| Metrics | xCOMET, MetricX, CometKiwi | Near chance at the EN→AR segment level |
| Unity text | RTLTMPro (hk1ll3r v4+) for uGUI/TMP; UI Toolkit with ATG for new UI on 6.3+ | No uGUI shaping before about 6.8 |
| Other engines | Unreal `FullShaping`; Godot TextServerAdvanced | Check settings and the font's GSUB/GPOS tables |
| Locale data | ICU/CLDR pinned at CLDR 48; Unity Localization Smart Strings | Snapshot-test every locale |
| Fonts | fontTools coverage check against all `ar` strings and the presentation forms | Atlas at least 512×512 for Arabic |
| Store | Google Play custom store listings by country; Apple Custom Product Pages and country-limited in-app events | iOS Custom Product Pages are not reachable organically |
| People | Native MSA linguists plus Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine reviewers; specialist vendors such as Tamatem, Altagram, LocalizeDirect | No public rate cards |

## Conclusion

The question "how do we localize for thirteen countries" turns out to be mostly a question of where the variation lives. Language variation is small and belongs in a thin overlay. Formatting, legal and content variation is large and belongs in versioned configuration that machines can test exhaustively. That split is what makes a 99 target affordable: the expensive human audit runs on one master and a few overlays, while thirteen countries' worth of digits, calendars, prices, ratings and feature flags become deterministic tests. It also changes how to read the current AI disappointment. The model is rarely the weakest link. The source strings, the missing context and the text renderer are, and fixing them upstream improves every language at once.

Two things remain unproven and call for experiments rather than assumptions. No public data shows that dialect copy beats MSA in push notifications, store listings or UA creatives in any of these countries, so the Gulf and Egyptian overlays should launch as A/B cells, with CTR, CVR and retention read per country. And no automatic metric yet tracks human judgment for Arabic at the segment level. That makes the publisher's own human-scored MQM samples an asset: logged consistently, they become the calibration set that lets a future judge model take over more of the triage. That is the only credible way to cut human review cost without giving up the 99.
